Working memory is a tiny desk: the more you spread out at once, the less you can actually process. Cognitive load theory often separates three types:

1. **Intrinsic load**: difficulty inherent in the task.
2. **Extraneous load**: burden from poor presentation, distraction, and nonessential information.
3. **Germane load**: effort that builds understanding and schemas.

## How Short Video Raises Extraneous Load

Jump cuts, captions, sound, and comment entry appear together; orienting is pulled away repeatedly. It feels like “just relaxing,” but working memory keeps clearing and rebuilding. Result: after closing the app, the desk is still messy, and long tasks feel heavy to start.

## How Training and Load Reduction Fit Together

- [N-Back](/en/tools/n-back/) practices updating information under control so you can sense working memory limits.
- [Schulte table](/en/tools/schulte/) demands ordered scanning, inhibiting the habit of glancing everywhere.
- Environment: single-task windows, fewer notifications—see [Environment Design](/en/methods/digital-environment/).

## Practical Principle

**Reduce extraneous load before you raise difficulty.** Much “slower reaction” is excess interference, not permanent ability loss. Clear the desk and performance often rebounds quickly.
