“Rewire your brain” is common in marketing and invites miracle expectations. A steadier science-facing line: **experience changes how efficiently specific circuits are used**. Piano, language, or scan practice makes those skills smoother—not a blanket “IQ upgrade.”

## Three Conditions for Effective Practice

1. **Enough repetition**: short, regular beats sporadic cramming.
2. **Clear feedback**: knowing right/wrong and timing to adjust strategy.
3. **Consolidation windows**: sleep and rest support memory consolidation—see [Sleep Rhythm and Performance](/en/principles/sleep-rhythm-performance/).

This site’s tools emphasize instant feedback and logged scores to meet the first two.

## Transfer: Training A Does Not Guarantee Mastery of B

Schulte gains mainly improve visual search and attention allocation; they do not automatically make you write faster. Transfer usually happens on **shared ingredients** (e.g., resisting interference, lower startup cost). Plans should bind tool practice to real tasks (reading, deep work)—see [Four-Week Integrated Plan](/en/plans/4-week-reset/).

## Scope Note

This article discusses learning and practice mechanisms only. It does **not** cover disease treatment, brain injury rehab, or clinical intervention. For health questions, consult qualified professionals.
