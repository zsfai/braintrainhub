Getting hooked on short video is not mysterious. A common **habit loop** is: cue → routine → reward. Short video makes reward **variable-ratio**: the next item may be dull or thrilling—uncertainty itself raises repeat probability.

## How the Loop Gets Built

- **Cue**: boredom, waiting in line, bedtime, notification badges.
- **Behavior**: swipe up, tap, autoplay chain.
- **Reward**: novelty, emotional swing, social signals, brief relief.

When most idle time goes to one loop, the brain gets faster at launching it automatically. That is learning, not a moral failing.

## Where “Sluggish Response” Comes From

The loop eats time and motivation that could start long tasks. Long tasks delay reward; in the short term they often lose to short video. Subjective experience becomes: low drive, restlessness, slow return to “real” work. Attention background: [Attention Systems Primer](/en/principles/attention-systems/).

## Scope Note

This covers common behavioral mechanisms and self-management—not diagnosis or treatment of addiction disorders. If use seriously disrupts life, seek qualified professional help.

## Actionable Interrupt Points

1. Weaken cues: remove home-screen entry, grayscale, bedtime limits.
2. Replace behavior: use a 5–10 minute [focus session](/en/tools/focus-timer/) startup in idle gaps.
3. Deliver alternative reward: after a short training block or reading, take real rest instead of scrolling again.

More steps: [Digital Minimalism](/en/methods/digital-minimalism/) and [Environment Design](/en/methods/digital-environment/).
